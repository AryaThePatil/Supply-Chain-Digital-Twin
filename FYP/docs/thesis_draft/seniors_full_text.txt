CHAPTER 1 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Supply chain consists of many parts. We can see that a product starts its journey right 
from the factory to the retailor and then to the customer goes through many points like 
wholesaler, distributor and then retailor. Warehouse management plays a key role in supply 
chain that helps in timely restocking, optimized storages are optimized, and efficient usage of 
resource. In today's world, the traditional manual system fails to address these needs because 
of non-adaptability and non-predictiveness. With the development of machine learning and 
reinforcement learning techniques we are able to develop intelligent warehouse systems. 
This is a project about a Warehouse Management System (WMS) using Deep Q-
Network (DQN) for inventory predication and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for 
restocking based on warehouse location. The aim is to develop a full stack application that 
not only tracks daily sales and inventory but also suggests smartly how much to restock and 
from which nearby warehouse. The website is developed on a full stack architecture (Node.js 
backend, MongoDB, Python ML, and HTML/JS frontend). 
 
1.1 Supply Chain Management 
 
1.1.1 Background 
 
The supply chain is the lifeline of modern commerce, that covers the complete process of 
producing and delivering goods from raw material sourcing to the final delivery to the 
consumers. Efficient supply chain management (SCM) is important to minimize costs of 
operations, improving customer satisfaction, and gaining a competitive advantage. 
Traditional SCM has relied on deterministic models, statistical forecasting, and rule-based 
systems to handle logistics, demand planning, and inventory management.

However, today’s real-world supply chains are increasingly dynamic and complex. 
Unpredictable shifts in customer demand, supplier delays, transportation disruptions, and 
geopolitical tensions challenge the robustness of traditional methods. As highlighted by 
Swaroop Reddy Gayam et al. [1], artificial intelligence (AI) techniques, particularly 
predictive maintenance, demand forecasting, and inventory optimization, are reshaping 
supply chain operations by enabling systems to predict disruptions and adapt proactively. 
The integration of AI not only enhances supply chain visibility but also enables dynamic 
decision-making, where models continuously learn from operational data to optimize 
inventory levels, minimize downtime, and align supply with fluctuating demand. 
Recent, advancement in reinforcement learning have made it possible for 
developing agents that can learn optimal policies from data. Deep Q-Networks (DQN) is 
used for environments with discrete action spaces like determining daily restock 
quantities, while Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is often used for tasks like 
selecting the best warehouse location by considering multiple factors such as how far it is 
distance, whether it is available, and time. 
 
Why is warehouse inventory management a challenging task? 
 
 
Dynamic Sales Behaviour: Customer demand varies daily and by season, making it 
difficult to maintain optimal inventory without the prediction on the inventory. 
 
Space Constraints: Warehouses have limited storage space, requiring careful allocation 
and restocking decisions per item. 
 
Multiple Supply Sources: Selecting the best restocking warehouse involves taking in 
consideration stock availability, delivery distance, and travel time, etc. 
 
Limitations of models: Traditional models struggle to handle the complex relationships 
between sales, inventory, and the patterns among them, machine learning-based methods 
are helpful in these cases.

1.1.2 Problem Statement 
 
Traditional inventory systems lack intelligent prediction capabilities, often resulting in 
overstocking or stockouts. Hence the problem identified is to efficiently handle the 
inventory management tasks using reinforcement learning. Our goal is to predict the next 
day's inventory requirement for each item using past sales and inventory data, and also to 
suggest the best nearby supplier warehouse for restocking. 
The problem can be framed into two parts: 
 Predicting inventory levels for each item in every warehouse using Deep Q-
Learning (DQN). 
 Restocking Location Selection using PPO based on item availability, distance, and 
travel duration. 
 
Why Reinforcement Learning (RL)? 
 
RL, an advanced machine learning method, that gives adaptive decision-making and helps 
optimize SCM processes by responding dynamically to changing conditions, learning from 
past outcomes, and continuously improving decisions. 
As outlined by Sutton et al. [2], RL frameworks excel when an agent must make a series of 
interdependent decisions under uncertainty, especially when the underlying system dynamics 
are unknown or too complex to model explicitly. Unlike traditional optimization techniques 
that rely on static datasets or predefined rules, RL agents develop strategies by exploring and 
exploiting the environment, gradually improving their policies through trial-and-error 
experiences. This ability to continuously adapt based on reward feedback makes RL 
particularly effective for real-world supply chains, where factors such as demand patterns, 
supplier reliability, and transportation logistics frequently fluctuate in unpredictable ways. 
It is best suited for sequential, feedback-driven environments like inventory management and 
supplier selection, where every action impacts future system states. 
Unlike traditional models, RL does not rely on predefined rules or static data sets, which 
make it ideal for managing the uncertainty and supply chain fluctuations.

1.1.3 Solution 
 
The proposed solution uses reinforcement learning techniques for autonomous decision-
making in warehouse environments: 
Inventory Prediction using DQN: Deep Q-Learning is a powerful technique in 
Reinforcement Learning (RL) that combines traditional Q-Learning with deep neural 
networks to manage complex environments with large or continuous state spaces. For each 
item, we train a DQN agent using past inventory and sales data. The agent learns how 
much to restock daily by optimizing rewards based on stock sufficiency and space 
utilization. 
Supplier Warehouse Selection using PPO: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a policy 
gradient algorithm in reinforcement learning developed by OpenAI. It is used to train 
agents safely and efficiently, especially in complex environments with high-dimensional 
state and action spaces. For selecting the best warehouse to restock from, we use the model 
so that it learns from features like stock availability at nearby warehouses, distance, and 
delivery duration. 
Both models are deployed through a full stack system: 
 Frontend (HTML/CSS/JS): Displays inventory data and lets users train and make 
predictions. 
 Backend (Node.js + Express): Handles routing and communication with 
MongoDB and Python ML scripts. 
 Machine Learning Layer (Python): Trains and predicts using DQN and PPO 
models, storing results in MongoDB.

LITERATURE SURVEY 
 
2.1 Traditional Inventory Management Techniques  
Traditional techniques of inventory handling have been the backbone of warehouse 
management for decades. These techniques mainly focus on maintaining the optimal stock 
levels, minimizing holding and shortage costs, and ensuring product availability.  As much 
as they are used across the globe, these approaches mainly operate on static rules and 
assumptions that make it difficult for them to adapt to the dynamic nature of supply chain 
environments. 
2.1.1 Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) 
The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model calculates the optimal order quantity that 
minimizes the total inventory management cost, balancing ordering and holding costs. It 
assumes that demand remains constant, lead times are stable, and holding costs do not 
vary over time. While EOQ offers a mathematically sound approach under idealized 
conditions, it often struggles in real-world scenarios characterized by fluctuating demand, 
multiple warehouses, inflationary pressures, and deteriorating goods. Recent studies, such 
as the work by Mallick et al. [3], have shown that the classical EOQ model requires 
significant adaptations to remain relevant in dynamic environments. Their research 
proposed a modified EOQ model that accounts for stock-dependent demand, inflation 
effects, and the deterioration of inventory over time. This highlights the limitations of the 
traditional EOQ model and reinforces the need for more adaptive and intelligent inventory 


=== PAGE 19 ===
9 
 
management strategies, such as those based on machine learning and reinforcement 
learning, especially in volatile supply chain networks. 
2.1.2 Just-In-Time (JIT) 
Just-in-Time (JIT) is a demand-driven inventory strategy where materials and products 
are replenished only as needed in the production process, minimizing holding costs to the 
bare minimum. Originally developed in Japan, particularly in the automobile sector by 
companies like Toyota, JIT focuses on reducing inventory waste, optimizing workflows, 
and enhancing operational efficiency. As discussed by Cheng and Podolsky [4], the JIT 
philosophy extends beyond mere inventory control, influencing production scheduling, 
supplier management, and overall supply chain coordination. While JIT significantly 
lowers storage costs and streamlines operations, it is heavily dependent on the reliability 
and punctuality of suppliers. Any disruption, delay, or inconsistency in the supply chain 
can severely impact production continuity, making JIT systems vulnerable in volatile or 
unpredictable environments. Therefore, while JIT offers substantial efficiency gains under 
stable conditions, it poses considerable risks if not supported by resilient and synchronized 
supply chain networks. 
2.1.3 Reorder Point (ROP) and Safety Stock Models 
The Reorder Point (ROP) model automatically triggers restocking when inventory falls 
below a predetermined threshold, ensuring that stock levels are maintained to meet 
customer demand. Safety stock is simultaneously maintained as a buffer against 
fluctuations in demand or lead time variability. As discussed by Teuku Faisal Umry et al. 
[5], ROP models are crucial in environments with random demand variations, particularly 
under a continuous review policy, which monitors inventory levels in real-time to avoid 
stockouts. However, despite their operational simplicity, traditional ROP models are non-
predictive and can lead to either stockouts or overstocking if not recalibrated frequently. 
Static ROP thresholds, when disconnected from real-time demand trends and lead time 
uncertainties, may result in significant financial inefficiencies such as excessive working 
capital being tied up or service level failures. This highlights the need for more dynamic 
and intelligent inventory management approaches, particularly in volatile supply chains.

2.1.4 Limitations of Traditional Techniques 
These models are normally based on static parameter assumption, such as the application 
of fixed rates of demand and some deterministic lead times. These models suffer from 
incorporating today's real time data, high market fluctuation, or between Warehouse 
relations. Traditional approaches cannot learn from historical data or dynamically change 
strategies, which is essential for managing modern supply chains. 
2.2 Machine Learning Management Techniques 
 
 
 
 
 
As data availability and computational capabilities increased, machine learning (ML) has 
become a valuable aid to enhance forecasting precision and operational-level decision-
making for inventory management.  ML-based inventory forecasting techniques offer the 
ability to see the complex, nonlinear relationships in data and they outperforming 
traditional methods in dynamic environments. 
2.2.1 Regression and Time Series Model 
 
Regression and time series models have long served as foundational techniques for 
forecasting inventory demand. Supervised learning algorithms such as Linear Regression, 
Decision Trees, Random Forests, and Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs) are 
commonly employed to predict future inventory requirements by utilizing historical sales 
data, seasonal patterns, and promotional activity as input features. Random Forests, an 
ensemble-based learning algorithm, aggregate predictions from multiple decision trees, 
thereby reducing the risk of overfitting and improving the model's generalization 
capabilities. Gradient Boosting Machines, on the other hand, sequentially build an 
ensemble of weak learners, with each tree correcting the errors made by the previous one, 
thereby creating a highly accurate predictive model. 
 
As highlighted by Akbar Abbaspour Ghadim Bonab [6], machine learning models like 
Random Forest and Gradient Boosting have demonstrated superior performance over 
traditional statistical models such as ARIMA, particularly when dealing with non-linear 
relationships and complex demand patterns in supply chain environments. The study 
emphasizes that while time series approaches provide useful trend analysis, machine

11
 
learning models offer higher predictive accuracy and adaptability by leveraging rich 
historical data and feature interactions. This advancement sets the stage for more dynamic 
inventory forecasting systems, especially critical in volatile and high-variability supply 
chains. 
 
2.2.2 Deep Learning Models 
 
Deep learning methods such as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Long Short-Term 
Memory (LSTM) networks, and Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) have shown 
significant promise in remembering long-term dependencies and sequential patterns in 
demand forecasting tasks. Unlike traditional models, deep learning architectures can 
automatically learn complex feature interactions and seasonality from raw time series 
data, making them highly effective for inventory and sales prediction. 
 
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a class of neural networks that are particularly 
suited for sequential data, where the prediction at a given time depends on previous 
observations. RNNs introduce internal memory through recurrent connections, allowing 
them to model temporal dependencies. However, standard RNNs struggle with capturing 
long-term dependencies due to issues like vanishing gradients. 
 
To overcome these limitations, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks were 
developed. LSTMs are a specialized form of RNNs designed to remember information 
over extended sequences. As discussed by Cristian Cracan [7], LSTM architectures are 
particularly effective in capturing complex temporal dynamics in retail sales and demand 
forecasting tasks. Cracan demonstrated that LSTM models outperform traditional 
statistical methods and even simple RNNs when dealing with real-world sales datasets 
characterized by seasonality, noise, and irregular demand patterns. The ability of LSTMs 
to selectively forget, remember, and update information across long sequences makes 
them highly suitable for future inventory forecasting in supply chain systems. However, 
despite their effectiveness, LSTMs can become computationally expensive and require extensive 
tuning of hyperparameters, and they may struggle with extremely long sequences or very large 
datasets where models like Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) or Transformers might 
perform better.

2.2.3 Advantages and Limitations 
 
Machine learning models provide adaptability, higher accuracy, and the ability to process 
a large number of variables these are the advantages and they also require a well-organized 
and properly labeled data and limitations are its decision-making ability and it is used for 
prediction rather than optimization. 
 
Although the machine learning models offer improvements over traditional methods, they 
still face challenges in automating decision-making and being seamlessly integrated with 
warehouse operations. 
 
2.3 Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Warehouse Management 
 
The agent takes the actions in the world and then it receives the feedback in the form of 
rewards or penalties based upon the outcomes of those actions. Then the feedback is used 
to modify the decision-making process of the agent so that it can improve and learn over 
time. In supervised learning, we have seen that the models are being trained over the 
labeled data, In RL models learn automatically by experimenting and implementing 
different tactics. 
In warehouse management, RL provides powerful tools to improve the various aspects of 
the operation such as inventory prediction, restocking, selecting the best warehouse 
location among all. By data-driven decision-making, RL can make the operation more 
efficient, reduce costs, and improve overall operational performance in supply chains, and 
making it well-suited for complex and dynamic environments to solve the problems of 
inventory management, warehouse allocation, and best warehouse selection among all. 
 
Deep Q-Network (DQN): The Deep Q-Network (DQN) is an optimized extension of 
traditional Q-learning that utilizes deep neural networks to approximate the Q-value 
function, making it scalable to problems with large and continuous state-action spaces. 
Instead of storing and updating a tabular Q-table, the DQN learns a function that maps 
state-action pairs to expected rewards through supervised learning, enabling it to 
generalize across high-dimensional environments. As demonstrated in the PyTorch 
DQN tutorial [8], techniques such as experience replay and target networks are

management, DQN offers significant advantages by efficiently predicting optimal 
inventory replenishment actions, even when faced with large, dynamic datasets 
representing inventory levels, lead times, and fluctuating demand patterns. 
 
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO): Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a 
policy-gradient reinforcement learning algorithm that has proven effective for solving 
complex decision-making tasks, particularly those involving continuous action spaces. 
Unlike value-based methods like DQN, PPO directly optimizes the policy by

As explained in the PyTorch PPO tutorial [9], PPO uses a clipped surrogate objective 
function that prevents large, destabilizing updates to the policy network, ensuring 
more gradual and consistent learning. This makes PPO particularly suitable for high-
variance environments such as warehouse management, where continuous restocking 
decisions and dynamic supplier selections are necessary. In this project, PPO 
facilitates adaptive decision-making under uncertainty, optimizing warehouse location 
selection in a way that traditional discrete-action models may struggle to handle.

conditions in real-time. 
 
2.3.1 Deep Q-Networks (DQN) for Inventory Forecasting 
 
      Deep Q-Networks (DQNs) are an advanced class of value-based reinforcement learning 
(RL) algorithms that successfully combine the best aspects of Q-learning and deep learning. 
In standard Q-learning, the agent maintains a Q-table that estimates the expected 
cumulative reward for taking a specific action in a particular state. However, this approach 
becomes impractical in large or continuous state-action spaces due to the exponential 
increase in dimensionality. Therefore, DQN replaces the static Q-table with a deep neural 
network that approximates the Q-value function, allowing the agent to generalize across 
similar states and actions.

In the domain of warehouse management, DQNs have emerged as a powerful tool for 
inventory prediction and restocking strategies. Warehouses typically operate in dynamic 
environments characterized by fluctuating customer demands, variable supplier lead times, 
and space constraints. As discussed by Ilaria Giannoccaro et al. [10] and further explored 
by Tao Zhou et al. [11], DQN-based systems enable autonomous agents to learn optimal 
replenishment policies through self-interaction in simulated or real environments. By 
engaging in trial-and-error learning, the agent continuously updates its strategy based on 
observed rewards, gradually adapting its choices to maximize long-term performance. 
 
A DQN-based inventory forecasting system typically processes input features such as 
current inventory levels, historical sales patterns, item pricing, and available storage 
capacity. The output action recommends the optimal quantity to reorder for each item at 
any given decision point. Over several training episodes, the agent learns to recognize 
complex patterns in demand data and adjusts its inventory ordering behaviour accordingly. 
This adaptability makes DQN particularly advantageous in high-volatility environments, 
where traditional static models like Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) or Reorder Point 
(ROP) often fail to maintain service levels or cost efficiency.  
 
2.3.2 Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for Restocking Location Selection 
 
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is an advanced reinforcement learning algorithm 
belonging to the policy-gradient family, designed to address the instability, inefficiency, and 
sensitivity to hyperparameters that often plague traditional policy-gradient methods. As 
introduced by Schulman et al. [12], PPO improves stability during training by using a 
clipped surrogate objective function, which restricts the extent to which the updated policy 
can deviate from the old policy at each step. This constraint ensures a more balanced trade-
off between exploration and stability, enabling more reliable convergence, particularly in 
environments with continuous and high-dimensional action spaces. 
 
In the context of warehouse management, PPO offers distinct advantages over value-based 
methods like Q-learning or Deep Q-Networks (DQNs), which estimate action-values 
indirectly and may suffer from unstable policy improvements in complex environments. 


=== PAGE 25 ===
15
 
Unlike DQNs, PPO directly learns a mapping from states to actions, enabling it to operate 
more effectively in settings where decisions involve continuous variables, such as 
prioritizing suppliers based on regional demand and operational constraints. As detailed by 
Engstrom et al. [13], careful attention to PPO's implementation details, such as appropriate 
choice of clipping parameters and batch sizes, further enhances its robustness and practical 
deployment in real-world applications. 
 
Moreover, PPO can be applied to restocking location prediction, wherein the agent must 
determine the optimal warehouse or distribution center to fulfill inventory restocking needs 
for different regions. This problem involves complex considerations such as transportation 
costs, regional demand variability, lead times, and current warehouse capacity — a high-
dimensional, dynamic decision space that PPO handles effectively. For instance, in a large 
distribution network, a well-trained PPO agent can not only predict which warehouse should 
fulfil an order but also determine the precise amount of inventory that should be moved 
from each source location to balance stock levels across the network. Such intelligent 
decision-making greatly enhances supply chain efficiency by reducing transportation costs, 
minimizing stockouts, and ensuring timely order fulfilment, which are critical objectives in 
modern logistics systems.

of overstocking and stockouts. In the long run, it learns to place proactive orders that 
accommodate changing demand and finite storage space.

4.4.2 PPO for Warehouse Location Selection 
 
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a reinforcement learning algorithm which is

Model Architecture

 Critic Network: The current state value is estimates by critic. This predicted value

expected long-term reward of selecting certain storage locations.

The PPO training loop consists of the following steps: 
 Action Selection: Based on the current state the agent selects an action (storage

 Reward Calculation: After selecting an action, the agent receives a reward based

 Policy Update: Using the surrogate objective function the policy is updated where

objective function is used to maintain the stability. 
 Critic Update: The critic network is updated by minimizing the loss between the 
predicted value and the actual return. 
 Repeated Iterations: The process is repeated for multiple episodes, where the 
model learns to optimize the storage locations over time by refining its policy.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
 
In this project, we designed and developed a full-stack warehouse management 
system that integrates machine learning algorithms with a practical web interface for 
efficient inventory control and location selection. We discover that supply chain 
management optimizations, where reward-based learning is very helpful, can be 
effectively demonstrated using reinforcement learning. The system uses Deep Q-Network 
(DQN) for dynamic inventory forecasting and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to 
choose the best possible warehouse among others based on restock scores, distance, and 
travel time. Also, we discussed the 2 main techniques used in RL to solve one of the main 
issues of Supply chain that is inventory management and warehouse management. 
 We used different techniques in Reinforcement Learning like Q-learning, DQN and 
PPO to improve the performance of the model shown on that website. Q-Learning, 
which provides a fundamental tabular approach to learning optimal policies in 
discrete environments. 
 Deep Q-Networks (DQN), which extend Q-learning by using neural networks for 
estimating Q-values, thus making it scalable to large and continuous spaces. 
 
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), an advanced policy gradient method that 
ensures stable and effective learning by limiting drastic policy improvements. 
The addition of these RL methods not only improved the performance of operations 
but also demonstrated the potential for change that AI can bring to the supply industry. As 
supply chains continuously becoming more and more complex and facing the challenges 
globally like demand uncertainty, transportation disruptions, and market unpredictability 
reinforcement learning stands out as a promising solution to all of this.

Future Work 
While the current system which we have developed demonstrates promising results, several 
enhancements can be made in it: 
1.Real-Time, Autonomous Supply Chains 
 The current implementation uses batch-based simulation for training. Future work 
may focus on integrating real-time data pipelines to allow the system to continuously 
learn and adapt from live warehouse operations. 
2. Integration with Digital Twins 
 Digital twins (virtual representation of our physical systems) that can simulate 
supply chain environments where RL agents can be trained safely. 
 RL agents interact with digital twins that enable testing of potential disruptions that 
can happen, fine-tuning of the processes, and prepare for rare but major events such 
as pandemics or natural disasters. 
3. Improved Reward Design 
 Reward functions can be further improved more by incorporating more business 
focused such as adding holding costs, stockout costs, or delivery deadlines to better 
reflect with real-world priorities that are there. 
4. Advanced Frontend Features 
The frontend interface can be further improved to include: 
 Real-time alerts when the inventory is low. 
 Interactive dashboards for ML model performance. 
5. Sustainability and Green Logistics 
RL can help reduce environmental impacts by: 
 Optimizing fuel-efficient routes. 
 Minimizing waste through better demand forecasting.
